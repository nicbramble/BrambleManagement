From your first listing to a deal you have checked.

Meta’s Muse can find Marketplace listings, make offers, negotiate, and help sell items. The builder above turns your numbers into a starting prompt. These are Nic.Buildz example workflows, not a promise of a discount or a completed sale.

Checked October 1, 2026. Meta describes Muse as available in the US and Canada. Access, permissions and screens can vary. This guide is for the Muse agent at muse.ai, not simply a Meta AI chat inside Facebook. Official references are linked below.

1. Open Muse and bring one listing

Open Muse with the button above and sign in. Copy your finished prompt from the builder, paste it into a new conversation, and add the Marketplace listing link. Starting with one item keeps the first negotiation easy to follow.

  • Buying: include the exact model, condition, accessories and listed price. Tell Muse what you need to verify before buying.
  • Selling: include honest photos, condition, known flaws, included accessories and your asking price. If you want a new listing, ask for a draft to review before posting.
  • Still searching? Tell Muse the item, city or general area, search radius and budget. Ask for three comparable listings with links before contacting anyone.

Ask Muse to open the listing. If it requests Facebook access, review the access request and use the sign-in flow it provides. Connect only what this task needs. If it cannot access a listing or conversation, paste the relevant text and use draft messages instead.

2. Set the numbers before the conversation

Buying: choose an opening offer and a maximum total you can comfortably pay. Include delivery or other costs in that maximum.

Selling: choose an asking price, a target price and the minimum you want to receive after any costs. Keep the minimum private.

Use comparable items in similar condition to ground the offer. An active listing shows what someone is asking; it does not prove what buyers actually paid. There is no magic discount percentage that works on every item.

Example: a desk is listed for $200. You open at $150 and cap your total at $180. A $190 counteroffer is still above your limit, even though it is below the asking price. These are practice numbers, not a valuation.

3. Choose drafts or a bounded negotiation

Draft messages for me to send: Muse writes the next message. You review it and send it yourself in Marketplace. This is a useful way to learn the flow.

Negotiate after I approve the plan: Muse first shows you the item, person, opening message and proposed approach. After you approve the plan and the access it needs, the prompt allows up to two counteroffer rounds with that person. Then Muse pauses with a summary.

Read the actual permission request before approving it. Prefer an approval limited to this task when offered. The instructions in a prompt are not a replacement for permission controls or checking what Muse has done.

Before approving a plan, confirm these four things:

  • It names the right listing and person.
  • It uses the right currency and your exact price limits.
  • It describes the condition truthfully and keeps messages polite.
  • It asks you before a final commitment, payment or pickup arrangement.

4. Review the reply and make one next move

When an offer comes back, ask Muse to summarize the current price, unresolved questions and next proposed reply. Decide whether to counter, accept after checking the details, or walk away.

Pause and summarize this Marketplace conversation. Show the latest offer, total cost or net proceeds, any unanswered condition questions, and how the offer compares with my private price limit. Recommend one next step and draft the exact reply. Do not send it or change my limits until I approve.

A short, specific message usually gives the other person more to respond to than “lowest price?” Ask about condition, propose a real number, and mention pickup flexibility only if it is true. If they decline, respect the answer.

5. Confirm the whole deal yourself

Before you commit, confirm the exact item, agreed price, condition, included parts and any delivery cost. Inspect or test the item as appropriate before paying. For local pickup, choose a suitable public meeting place when practical and agree on a time you can actually make.

Keep your home address and private contact details out of the negotiation prompt. Review any information Muse proposes sharing. Never send a verification code to a buyer or seller, and treat pressure to pay an advance deposit or move to a suspicious payment link as a reason to pause.

Stop negotiating and give me the final proposal: item and listing link, agreed or proposed price, fees or delivery costs, condition and included accessories, open questions, and proposed pickup arrangements. Clearly distinguish confirmed facts from suggestions. Do not accept, pay, share my address, confirm pickup or mark anything sold until I explicitly approve each relevant action.

Done with this item? Tell Muse to stop the task and any related monitoring. Review its activity and remove access you no longer need. A successful negotiation ends with a deal you understand and actually want.
